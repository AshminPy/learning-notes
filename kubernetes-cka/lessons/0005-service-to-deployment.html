Service → Deployment

You already wired a Service to a bare Pod. Now do the same for a Deployment. Same mechanism, one layer up.

Simple example: a Service is like a receptionist who only reads name tags. It doesn't care who hired the person wearing the tag — Pod, Deployment, whatever. It just matches tags (labels) to send calls (traffic).

Where the labels live

A Service matches spec.selector against Pod labels. Nothing else. In a Deployment, those Pod labels sit inside the template, not at the top:

apiVersion: apps/v1
kind: Deployment
metadata:
  name: nginx-deployment
spec:
  replicas: 3
  selector:
    matchLabels:
      app: nginx
  template:
    metadata:
      labels:
        app: nginx      # <- this is what a Service selector matches
    spec:
      containers:
        - name: nginx
          image: nginx:1.14.2

The Service selector points at spec.template.metadata.labels — not the Deployment's own metadata.labels, and not spec.selector.matchLabels.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. Does a Service care whether Pods came from a Deployment, a ReplicaSet, or were created bare?

No. It only matches labels — it has no field that references who created the Pod.

Q2. During a rolling update, which Pods does a Service send traffic to?

Any Pod that matches the selector and is Ready — old and new Pods can both get traffic mid-rollout.

Now write your own

Write a ClusterIP Service that selects the Pods from the Deployment above.

  • a selector matching app: nginx
  • a ports entry with port and targetPort

Reveal the reference answer

apiVersion: v1
kind: Service
metadata:
  name: nginx-service
spec:
  selector:
    app: nginx
  ports:
    - protocol: TCP
      port: 80
      targetPort: 80

kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/services-networking/service

Recap
  • A Service matches spec.selector against Pod labels — it has no idea a Deployment exists.
  • A Deployment's Pod labels live at spec.template.metadata.labels.
  • A Service routes to any matching Pod that is Ready, even mid-rollout.
