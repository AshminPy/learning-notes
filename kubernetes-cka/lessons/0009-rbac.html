RBAC — ServiceAccount, Role, RoleBinding

Every manifest so far assumed permissions just work. They don't. RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) is the layer that says who is allowed to do what.

Simple example: think of RBAC like a keycard system. A ServiceAccount is the badge a Pod wears. A Role is the list of doors a badge type may open. A RoleBinding is what actually hands the badge those keys — no binding, no access, no matter what the Role allows.

The three pieces

A Role lists what's allowed:

apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: Role
metadata:
  name: pod-reader
rules:
  - apiGroups: [""]
    resources: ["pods"]
    verbs: ["get", "list"]
  • ServiceAccount — an identity a Pod can act as, instead of you
  • Role — which verbs are allowed on which resources, in one namespace (shown above)
  • RoleBinding — grants the Role to a subject (ServiceAccount, User, or Group)

ClusterRole / ClusterRoleBinding are the same two objects, just cluster-scoped instead of namespaced.

RBAC is additive only. There are no deny rules. A missing grant means no access — full stop.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. What actually grants a Role to someone — as opposed to just defining what it allows?

A RoleBinding (or ClusterRoleBinding). The Role only defines permissions — the binding attaches it to a subject.

Q2. What command checks if ServiceAccount ci-bot in namespace ci can delete pods — without actually testing it?

kubectl auth can-i delete pods --as=system:serviceaccount:ci:ci-bot -n ci

Now write your own

Write three manifests by hand (no copy-paste) that together let a workload read Pods and nothing else:

  • a ServiceAccount, any name, in a namespace of your choice
  • a Role in the same namespace allowing get and list on pods only
  • a RoleBinding connecting the two

Apply all three, then confirm with kubectl auth can-i before checking the answer below.

Reveal the reference answer

apiVersion: v1
kind: ServiceAccount
metadata:
  name: pod-reader-sa
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: Role
metadata:
  name: pod-reader
rules:
  - apiGroups: [""]
    resources: ["pods"]
    verbs: ["get", "list"]
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: RoleBinding
metadata:
  name: read-pods
subjects:
  - kind: ServiceAccount
    name: pod-reader-sa
roleRef:
  kind: Role
  name: pod-reader
  apiGroup: rbac.authorization.k8s.io

Recap
  • ServiceAccount = an identity a Pod acts as. Role = allowed verbs on resources. RoleBinding = what grants the Role.
  • ClusterRole / ClusterRoleBinding are the same pair, cluster-scoped.
  • RBAC is additive only — no deny rules, and no grant means no access.
