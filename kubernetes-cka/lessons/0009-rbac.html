RBAC — ServiceAccount, Role, RoleBinding

Every manifest so far assumed permissions just work. They don't — and Lesson 6 already flagged why this matters: a Secret's real protection is who can read it, not its encoding. RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) is the layer that decides who is allowed to do what to the API.

Mental model: a keycard system. A ServiceAccount is the badge a Pod wears. A Role is the list of doors a badge type may open. A RoleBinding is what actually hands the badge those keys. That predicts something exam candidates trip on constantly: a perfectly correct Role, with no RoleBinding pointing at it, grants nothing at all — a defined permission and a granted permission are two different objects.

Where this fits

Cluster └ Node └ Pod └ Labels
                          └ ServiceAccount   ← you are here (a Pod's identity for API calls)
Role            (what's allowed — verbs on resources)
   ▲
   │  attached via
RoleBinding     (subject: ServiceAccount / User / Group)
   ▲
   │  used by
Pod  spec.serviceAccountName

The three pieces

apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: Role
metadata:
  name: pod-reader
rules:
- apiGroups: [""]
  resources: ["pods"]
  verbs: ["get", "list"]
  • ServiceAccount — an identity a Pod can act as, instead of you
  • Role — which verbs are allowed on which resources, in one namespace
  • RoleBinding — grants the Role to a subject

ClusterRole/ClusterRoleBinding are the same two objects, cluster-scoped instead of namespaced.

Why it's built this way: splitting "what's allowed" (Role) from "who gets it" (RoleBinding) lets one Role definition (e.g. pod-reader) be reused across many teams/ServiceAccounts without rewriting the rules each time — the same separation-of-concerns pattern Lesson 7 used for storage (PV vs. PVC).

RBAC is additive only. There are no deny rules. A missing grant means no access — full stop.

Try it

Q1. What actually grants a Role to someone — as opposed to just defining what it allows?

A RoleBinding (or ClusterRoleBinding). The Role only defines permissions — the binding attaches it to a subject.

Now write your own, by hand: a ServiceAccount, a Role allowing only get/list on pods, and a RoleBinding connecting them. Apply all three, verify with kubectl auth can-i, then try before revealing.

Reveal the reference answer

apiVersion: v1
kind: ServiceAccount
metadata:
  name: pod-reader-sa
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: Role
metadata:
  name: pod-reader
rules:
- apiGroups: [""]
  resources: ["pods"]
  verbs: ["get", "list"]
---
apiVersion: rbac.authorization.k8s.io/v1
kind: RoleBinding
metadata:
  name: read-pods
subjects:
- kind: ServiceAccount
  name: pod-reader-sa
roleRef:
  kind: Role
  name: pod-reader
  apiGroup: rbac.authorization.k8s.io

Common mistake: writing a correct Role and assuming it does something on its own — nothing is granted until a RoleBinding names it.

A controlled failure

kubectl auth can-i delete pods --as=system:serviceaccount:default:pod-reader-sa

Predict first: yes or no? Observe: no — the Role above only grants get/list, and RBAC being additive-only means there's nothing to accidentally grant delete. Compare against a verb it does have:

kubectl auth can-i get pods --as=system:serviceaccount:default:pod-reader-sa

Production best practices

Production note: give every workload its own dedicated ServiceAccount with the narrowest Role it needs — never run production Pods under the default ServiceAccount, which typically has no useful permissions but is still a shared identity every unconfigured Pod in the namespace inherits.

Exam & interview notes

Core to Cluster Architecture, Installation & Configuration (25%).

Interview Q: "A ServiceAccount has a correctly-written Role for the access it needs, but calls still get denied. What's the first thing you check?" A: Whether a RoleBinding (or ClusterRoleBinding) actually attaches that Role to the ServiceAccount — a Role by itself grants nothing.

Recap
  • ServiceAccount = an identity a Pod acts as. Role = allowed verbs on resources. RoleBinding = what grants the Role.
  • ClusterRole/ClusterRoleBinding are the same pair, cluster-scoped.
  • RBAC is additive only — no deny rules, and no grant means no access.
