Service → Pod (ClusterIP)

Pods come and go — they get rescheduled, and get a new IP every time. A Service is a stable address that always finds the right Pods, by matching labels.

Simple example: think of a Service like a company's front-desk phone number. Staff (Pods) come and go, move desks, change extensions — but the front-desk number always routes you to whoever currently has the job.

What's inside a Service manifest

apiVersion: v1
kind: Service
metadata:
  name: my-nginx-svc
spec:
  type: ClusterIP
  selector:
    app: my-nginx
  ports:
    - port: 80
      targetPort: 80

Key fields:

  • spec.type — ClusterIP is the default: reachable only inside the cluster
  • spec.selector — must exactly match a Pod's metadata.labels, or the Service finds nothing
  • spec.ports — the port the Service listens on, and the port to forward to on the Pod

No selector match, no traffic — the Service exists but is silently empty. Inside the cluster, reach it by DNS: <service>.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. You create a Service and see no errors. What command tells you whether it actually found any Pods?

kubectl get endpoints <service-name> — an empty list means the selector matched nothing.

Now write your own

Write a ClusterIP Service for the Lesson 2 Pod — by hand. It needs:

  • a selector matching the labels on your Lesson 2 Pod
  • a port forwarding to the port your container listens on

Recap
  • A Service = a stable IP + DNS name that load-balances to Pods matching its selector.
  • spec.selector must exactly match the Pod's metadata.labels.
  • kubectl get endpoints <service> checks whether it actually found Pods.
