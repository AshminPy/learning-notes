NetworkPolicy

Service, Ingress, and RBAC all control who can reach an address or call an API. NetworkPolicy is different — it controls raw Pod-to-Pod traffic, at the packet level.

Simple example: with zero NetworkPolicy objects, every Pod can talk to every other Pod, on any port, in any namespace. This is the opposite of a firewall — Kubernetes starts wide open, and a NetworkPolicy is what you write to close paths.

The shape of a policy

apiVersion: networking.k8s.io/v1
kind: NetworkPolicy
metadata:
  name: allow-frontend
spec:
  podSelector:
    matchLabels:
      app: nginx
  policyTypes:
    - Ingress
  ingress:
    - from:
        - podSelector:
            matchLabels:
              role: frontend-client
  • podSelector — which Pods this policy applies to (empty {} = all Pods in the namespace)
  • policyTypes — Ingress, Egress, or both — which direction this policy governs
  • ingress.from / egress.to — the allow-list: podSelector, namespaceSelector, or ipBlock

Put podSelector and namespaceSelector in the same list entry and they AND together. List them as separate entries and they OR. One indent level flips the meaning.

The trap: a NetworkPolicy does nothing unless the cluster's CNI implements it. Apply it, and kubectl get shows it — but if the CNI doesn't enforce NetworkPolicy, traffic flows exactly as before, with no error anywhere.

Once any policy selects a Pod for a direction, that direction becomes deny-by-default except what's explicitly allowed. Multiple policies on the same Pod are additive (OR) — never AND.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. With no NetworkPolicy anywhere in the cluster, what's the default Pod-to-Pod traffic behavior?

Fully open — every Pod can reach every other Pod, any port, any namespace.

Q2. Two NetworkPolicies both select the same Pod for Ingress, each allowing a different source. Do they AND or OR?

OR. The Pod accepts anything allowed by either policy — policies never narrow each other.

Now write your own

Write a NetworkPolicy by hand (no copy-paste) that:

  • applies to Pods labeled app: nginx
  • governs Ingress only
  • allows traffic only from Pods labeled role: frontend-client, on port 80

Apply it, then test with a throwaway Pod that has the label, and one that doesn't.

Reveal the reference answer

apiVersion: networking.k8s.io/v1
kind: NetworkPolicy
metadata:
  name: nginx-allow-frontend
spec:
  podSelector:
    matchLabels:
      app: nginx
  policyTypes:
    - Ingress
  ingress:
    - from:
        - podSelector:
            matchLabels:
              role: frontend-client
      ports:
        - protocol: TCP
          port: 80

Recap
  • Default with no NetworkPolicy: every Pod reaches every Pod — the opposite of a firewall's default-deny.
  • A NetworkPolicy does nothing unless the CNI implements it — no error either way.
  • podSelector + policyTypes pick the Pods and direction; multiple policies are additive (OR), never AND.
