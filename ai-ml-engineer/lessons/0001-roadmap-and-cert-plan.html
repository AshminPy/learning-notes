Your roadmap: zero to AI/ML engineer job

You want a job as an AI/ML Engineer. You currently know zero code and zero AI. That gap closes in stages, not in one leap. This lesson is the map — read it once now, come back to it whenever you feel lost.

The one thing to remember: nobody hires you because you finished a course. They hire you because you can build something and explain it. Every lesson from here on either teaches you a skill or helps you build a project you can show in an interview.

The 10 phases

34 short lessons, grouped into 10 phases. You don't need to memorize this — it's on the roadmap tracker, which you can check off as you go.

Why this order

You can't learn machine learning without being able to code, and you can't code AI without knowing what a variable is. So: Python first (Phase B-C), then just enough math to understand what the algorithms are doing (Phase D), then the algorithms themselves (Phase E onward). Skipping ahead to "build a neural network" before Phase B will only frustrate you — trust the order.

The certifications — and why these ones

A certificate alone doesn't get you hired, but a well-chosen one gets your resume past filters and gives you a study structure. After checking each certification's official page directly, here's the plan:

  1. DeepLearning.AI Machine Learning Specialization (Andrew Ng) — free to audit. This is the theory backbone for Phases D-G. Most respected "did you actually learn this" signal among engineers.
  2. AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) — $100, no prerequisites, ~90 minutes. Your fastest resume-visible badge. Slot the exam in around Phase I.
  3. IBM AI Engineering Professional Certificate — hands-on: scikit-learn, Keras, PyTorch, TensorFlow, computer vision, NLP. This is where Phases E-H get their hands-on depth and your portfolio projects come from.

Not doing Azure AI Fundamentals (AI-900) — checked Microsoft's own certification page directly, and that exam has already moved to AI-901 as of April 2026. Recommending the old exam code would be stale advice.

Later — not now — there's a stretch goal: AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer – Associate. AWS itself recommends about a year of hands-on experience first, so this comes after you're working or deep into the IBM certificate — not on day one.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. Which phase comes right before "Core Machine Learning," and why does it need to come first?

Phase D, Math & Stats. ML algorithms are described using vectors, matrices, and probability — you need just enough of that vocabulary before the algorithms make sense, otherwise Phase E is just memorizing steps.

Q2. Name the certification you're explicitly not pursuing, and why.

Azure AI Fundamentals (AI-900) — it has already been replaced by exam AI-901 on Microsoft's own certification page, so studying for "AI-900" by name would be out of date.
