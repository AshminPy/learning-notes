GCP PCA — Lesson 19 of 28

Technical processes

A Cloud Architect doesn't just pick services — they're expected to advise on the process around them: how code moves from idea to production, and how a team learns from what breaks. This is process knowledge, not a GCP product list.

Simple example: the SDLC is the assembly line; CI/CD (Lesson 18) is one station on it. This lesson is the rest of the line — planning, testing discipline, and what happens after something breaks.

SDLC — the phases you're advising on

Plan → build → test → deploy → operate → learn, then repeat. The exam tests whether you can place a given practice in the right phase — e.g. a canary rollout is a deploy-phase practice, a blameless postmortem is a learn-phase practice.

Testing & validation, beyond CI

Lesson 18 covered running tests as a pipeline stage. The process question here is what to validate before something ships: does it meet the functional spec, does it hold up under expected load, does a rollback actually work if triggered. "We have tests" and "we validated the rollback path" are different claims — the exam rewards recognizing that gap.

Troubleshooting & root-cause analysis

The practice Google's own SRE discipline is built on: a blameless postmortem after any significant incident — reconstruct the timeline, find the root cause (not just the trigger), and produce concrete follow-up actions. "Blameless" isn't a soft-skills nicety on the exam — it's the mechanism that makes people report problems honestly instead of hiding them.

Exam trap: a root cause is not "the server ran out of memory." That's the trigger. The root cause is why nothing caught it earlier — no alert, no capacity plan, no load test at that scale. If an answer option stops at the trigger, it's usually the wrong one.

Service catalog

A curated, pre-approved set of self-service deployable templates — so teams can spin up "the approved way to deploy a Postgres-backed API" without re-inventing networking, IAM, and monitoring from scratch each time. The mechanism varies (Terraform modules, a private Cloud Marketplace catalog), but the underlying goal is always the same: standardize the golden path, reduce one-off risky configurations.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. After an outage, the writeup says "root cause: a Pod ran out of memory." Is that a complete root-cause analysis?

No — that's the trigger. A real RCA asks why it wasn't caught first: no memory alert, no load test at that traffic level, no autoscaling configured. The trigger explains what happened; the root cause explains why the system let it happen.

Quiz — Lesson 19

Q1. Which SDLC phase does a canary rollout belong to?

A. Deploy — it's a rollout strategy, not a design decision or a post-incident practice.

Q2. Why does Google's SRE practice insist postmortems be "blameless"?

A. Blame drives incidents underground — people minimize or hide what happened. Blameless culture is what makes root-cause data trustworthy in the first place.

Recap
  • SDLC = plan → build → test → deploy → operate → learn — place a given practice in its phase.
  • A root cause explains why a failure wasn't caught, not just what triggered it.
  • Blameless postmortems exist to keep incident data honest, not as a courtesy.
  • A service catalog standardizes the "approved way" to deploy something, reducing one-off risk.
