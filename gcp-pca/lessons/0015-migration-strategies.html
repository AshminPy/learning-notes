Migration strategies: rehost, replatform, refactor

Every migration to GCP fits one of three levels of change. The exam wants you to match the strategy to the deadline and goal stated in the scenario — not to whichever option sounds most advanced.

Simple example: think of moving house. Lift-and-shift is throwing your boxes straight in the truck. Replatform is packing smarter — new shelves, same stuff. Refactor is throwing half your stuff out and buying furniture built for the new place.

Lift and Shift (Rehost)

Move the workload with little or no change. Fastest option, uses skills your team already has. The workload still isn't optimized for cloud once it lands.

Replatform

Lift it, then make targeted upgrades — swap a self-managed database for Cloud SQL, containerize without a full rewrite. Some cloud-native wins, without a redesign.

Refactor (Rearchitect)

Redesign the workload to actually use cloud capabilities — microservices, managed services throughout. Best long-term result, biggest short-term effort.

Compare

StrategyEffortRiskExam picks this when...
Lift and ShiftLowLow — known-working systemHard deadline, data center exit, requirements unclear
ReplatformMediumMedium — new managed servicesModerate timeline, wants managed-service wins
RefactorHighHigher near-term, lower long-term"Long-term cost" or "cloud-native scale" stated, no deadline
Exam trap: a hard deadline ("data center lease ends in 6 weeks") always points at Lift and Shift, even when Refactor looks like the objectively better long-term design. The scenario is testing time-to-exit, not architecture. Refactor is a real best practice — just for a different question, one with no deadline pressure.

Migration Center — assessing before you pick a strategy

Before choosing rehost/replatform/refactor, Migration Center is Google's tool for the assessment step: it discovers your existing on-prem/other-cloud footprint, sizes it, and estimates GCP cost across different target configurations — turning "we think we have about 200 VMs" into an actual inventory with a cost estimate attached. If a scenario mentions an unclear or undocumented existing environment as the blocker, Migration Center is the "assess first" answer, before any of the three strategies above get chosen.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. A scenario has a hard 8-week data center exit deadline and no stated cost goal. Which strategy?

B. The deadline is the constraint keyword. Refactor is a real strategy, just not the one this scenario is testing.

Q2. A team wants managed-service wins (e.g. Cloud SQL) without a full rewrite, on a moderate timeline. Which strategy?

C. Replatform is exactly this: targeted upgrades, no ground-up redesign.

Q3. Which strategy carries the highest up-front effort but the lowest long-term cost?

C. Refactor redesigns for cloud-native — most work now, best TCO once it's done.

Recap
  • Lift and Shift = move as-is, fast, low risk, not optimized.
  • Replatform = lift it, then upgrade the parts that benefit most.
  • Refactor = redesign for cloud-native, highest effort, best long-term.
  • A hard deadline in the scenario always points at Lift and Shift.
  • Migration Center is the assessment step — inventory and cost estimate — before picking a strategy.
