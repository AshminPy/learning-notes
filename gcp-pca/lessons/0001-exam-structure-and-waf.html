GCP PCA — Session 1 of 10 (Day 1)

Exam structure, the Well-Architected Framework, and how Google writes questions

You already know cloud architecture — AWS, GKE, Terraform, IAM, this is your daily job. This session isn't teaching you cloud. It's teaching you this specific exam's shape: what it actually weighs, the lens every answer gets filtered through, and the trick to eliminating 2 of 4 options before you've fully worked the problem.

1. The real exam (verified against the official guide, not memory)

Duration2 hours
Questions50–60, multiple choice + multiple select
Cost$200

Case studies matter more than you'd think. Up to 30% of questions reference one of: Altostrat Media, Cymbal Retail, EHR Healthcare, KnightMotives Automotive. Before your exam, skim each one on the official guides page — 15 minutes now saves you re-reading a full case study cold under time pressure. Fact from the guide: several of these companies are already using Google's generative AI solutions — don't assume a case study company is AI-averse just because the scenario reads "traditional retailer."

3. The Well-Architected Framework — the lens behind "BEST" answers

Nearly every question with 2+ technically-valid options is really asking: which one best fits a specific pillar the scenario is hinting at? Six pillars (verified from the official framework docs — note this is 6, not 5, a recent addition):

PillarWhat it's really asking
Operational ExcellenceDoes this minimize ongoing ops burden on your team?
Security, Privacy & ComplianceLeast privilege, encryption, data residency respected?
ReliabilitySurvives a zone/region failure at the stated SLA?
Cost OptimizationBusiness value per dollar — not just "cheapest"
Performance OptimizationLatency/throughput tuned to the actual workload
SustainabilityRegion/resource choice minimizes carbon footprint

Exam trap: an answer can be technically correct and still be wrong on this exam, because it optimizes the wrong pillar for the scenario. A self-managed VM cluster with custom HA logic is "correct" — but if the scenario says "small team, minimize operations," the managed/serverless option wins even if it's marginally more expensive or less flexible.

4. Elimination algorithm — do this before you fully solve the question

  1. Underline the constraint keywords in the scenario: "minimal ops," "strict compliance," "existing on-prem," "unpredictable traffic," "lowest cost." These name the pillar being tested.
  2. Eliminate options that violate that pillar outright — even if they're technically functional. A compliant-but-overkill answer and a cheap-but-non-compliant answer both die here.
  3. Eliminate options solving the wrong problem — e.g. the scenario asks about data consistency and an option only addresses cost. PCA questions often include one answer that's a real GCP best practice, just for a different scenario.
  4. Between the last two: default to the option requiring less custom engineering, unless the scenario explicitly demands the control that only the more complex option provides (e.g. multi-region active-active, customer-managed keys).

Try it — before reading the answer

Scenario: "A retail company needs to store product images. They have a small ops team and want the cheapest option that still supports global low-latency reads." Two candidate answers: (A) Cloud Storage with a multi-region bucket, (B) a Compute Engine VM running an NFS server they manage. Using the algorithm above, which do you eliminate first and why?

Eliminate B first — step 2: "small ops team" is the constraint keyword, and self-managing an NFS server on a VM directly violates Operational Excellence (patching, scaling, HA are now your job). Cloud Storage is managed, has multi-region built in, and is the standard answer for "global low-latency object reads, minimal ops."

Quiz — Session 1

Q1. How many questions are on the standard PCA exam?

A. 50–60, per the official certification page. B/C/D are plausible-sounding but wrong — Google doesn't publish a range that wide, and guessing here costs you pacing math on exam day (2 hours ÷ ~55 questions ≈ 2 min/question).

Q2. Which exam section carries the highest weight?

A. Section 1 (Design & plan) is 25%, the single largest section. B (Implementation, 12.5%) and C (Ops excellence, 12.5%) are each half the weight. D (15%) is close but still lower. This is why sessions 2–4 (compute/storage/network selection) get the most of your remaining time.

Q3. A question hinges on "least privilege" and "customer-managed encryption keys." Which Well-Architected pillar is being tested?

A. Least privilege and key management are textbook Security/Privacy/Compliance signals. None of B/C/D relate to access control or encryption — if you see those keywords, don't get pulled toward a cost- or performance-framed distractor answer.

Q4. A scenario has a strict budget constraint. Two answers are both technically correct. One requires your team to own ongoing patching and scaling. Per the elimination algorithm, what do you do?

A. Step 4 of the algorithm: between two valid answers, default to less custom engineering/ops burden — that's also the cheaper option once you count engineer-hours, which is what "strict budget" is really asking about. B/C/D describe properties that aren't the deciding factor here.

Q5. What should you assume about the fictitious companies in PCA case studies?

A. Directly from the official guide: "Several of the businesses are using Google Cloud's generative AI solutions to solve real-world challenges." Don't assume a case study is legacy/on-prem-only just because its industry (retail, automotive) sounds traditional.

Q6. An option is technically valid but means your team owns ongoing patching. No explicit budget or compliance constraint is stated. What's the default move?

A. Absent an explicit reason to hand-roll infrastructure, Google's implicit bias (and Operational Excellence pillar) favors the managed service. This is a general default, not a rule — an explicit requirement (e.g. custom kernel modules) can override it.

Q7. Which section covers SDLC, CI/CD, and stakeholder management together?

A. Section 4 splits into 4.1 (technical processes: SDLC, CI/CD, troubleshooting) and 4.2 (business processes: stakeholder management, change management). It's the section people underestimate because it feels "soft," but it's 15% — more than Implementation or Ops Excellence alone.

Q8. How do Section 5 (Managing implementation, 12.5%) and Section 6 (Ops excellence, 12.5%) compare in weight?

A. Both are 12.5% per the official guide — an easy fact to misremember under pressure, and exactly the kind of thing a distractor answer will bait you on.
