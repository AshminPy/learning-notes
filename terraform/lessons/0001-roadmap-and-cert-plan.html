Your roadmap: zero to Terraform expert

You want to be able to open a real cloud account, describe the infrastructure it needs in code, and run that code with confidence — on a real team, against real production accounts. You currently know zero Terraform. That gap closes in stages, not in one leap. This lesson is the map — read it once now, come back to it whenever you feel lost.

The one thing to remember: the goal of this course is production confidence, not a certificate. The HashiCorp Certified Terraform Associate exam shows up later as a checkpoint — a way to prove to yourself (and an employer) that the fundamentals are solid — but passing it is not the finish line. Being able to write, review, and safely operate Terraform on a real team is the finish line.

Why this order

Every phase is a prerequisite for the one after it — skipping ahead just means backfilling later, so trust the sequence:

  • You can't learn state (Phase D) before you've applied a resource (Phase B). State is Terraform's record of what it already created. Until you've run terraform apply yourself and watched a resource come into existence, "a JSON file that tracks reality" is just an abstract sentence.
  • You can't learn modules (Phase E) before you understand resources and variables (Phases B-C). A module is just a reusable wrapper around resources, variables, and outputs. If you don't know what's being wrapped, you'll copy module syntax without understanding what it buys you — and you won't know when NOT to build one.
  • You can't safely use workspaces and environments (Phase F) before you understand state (Phase D). Workspaces exist to isolate state between environments. Without the state mental model first, workspace mistakes look mysterious instead of obvious.
  • You can't reason about production concerns (Phase J) before you've seen the full lifecycle (Phases B-I). Least-privilege IAM, blast radius, and rollback only make sense once you've felt what it's like to run plan/apply/destroy, break state, write a module, and wire up CI/CD. Production patterns are what experienced engineers do differently once they've already made the beginner mistakes — so you make those mistakes first, safely, in this course.
  • The certification (Phase K) comes near the end, not the start. The exam's 8 official domains span IaC concepts through HCP Terraform — content you won't have touched yet in Phase A. Studying for it before Phase J would mean memorizing terms with no hands-on grounding underneath them.

The certification — and why it's a milestone, not the goal

Checked directly against HashiCorp's own certification page (2026-07-30) — here's what's actually true about the HashiCorp Certified: Terraform Associate right now:

  • Exam code: 004
  • Tests: Terraform 1.12
  • Format: multiple choice, delivered online-proctored
  • Duration: 1 hour
  • Cost: $70.50 USD, plus locally applicable taxes and fees (no free retake included)
  • Prerequisites: none required — basic terminal skills and an understanding of on-premises/cloud architecture are recommended, and professional experience is recommended but not mandatory
  • Validity: 2 years before recertification is needed

This course places the exam at Phase K — around lesson 34-37 — deliberately late. A certificate that isn't backed by hands-on scars (a broken state file you fixed yourself, a module you wrote and later regretted, a CI pipeline you wired up) is a badge with nothing behind it. By the time you sit the exam here, you'll have already done the real work; the exam just confirms it.
