Job Control & Process Management

A process launched from your shell is tied to that shell session by default — close the terminal, and the process gets SIGHUP and usually dies with it. Job control lets you run things in the background and manage whether they survive your session ending.

Simple example: long-task & starts it in the background of your current session — but log out, and it still dies. nohup long-task & makes it immune to the hangup signal, so it survives you logging off entirely.

The toolkit

CommandWhat it does
cmd &Run in the background of the current session
jobsList background jobs in the current session
fg %1Bring job 1 back to the foreground
bg %1Resume a stopped job in the background
nohup cmd &Immune to SIGHUP — survives you logging out
disownDetach an already-running background job from the shell, so closing the shell won't touch it

For anything long-running you actually care about (a real deploy, a migration), a terminal multiplexer (tmux or screen) is usually the better tool — it keeps the whole session alive, not just one process, and you can reattach to it later.

Try it — before looking below

Q1. You start a 3-hour data migration and need to close your laptop for the night. What's wrong with just running it plain and backgrounding it with &?

A plain background job is still tied to your SSH session — closing the laptop (or the connection dropping) sends SIGHUP and kills it. You need nohup, disown, or a multiplexer like tmux for it to actually survive.

Now try it yourself

nohup sleep 300 &         # start something that outlives your session
jobs                       # see it listed
disown -a                  # detach all jobs from this shell
exit                       # the process keeps running even after you log out

Source

GNU Bash Manual — Job Control — the official reference.

Recap
  • Background jobs (&) are still tied to your session by default — they die on logout via SIGHUP.
  • nohup and disown both detach a process from that hangup signal.
  • For anything you'll want to check on or reattach to later, tmux/screen beats plain backgrounding.
If a job dies the moment you disconnect and you're sure you backgrounded it correctly, check whether it was actually nohup'd — a common, easy-to-miss gap.
